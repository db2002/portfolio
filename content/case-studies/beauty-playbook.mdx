## The beauty industry thrives on trends... but trust is fading.

The beauty content space isn't short on opinions, but on credible ones. Platforms are either very by-the-book with no real personality, or filled with brand deals and paid content. **Beauty Playbook wanted to sit in the gap: for beauty pros, by beauty pros.**

## Understanding the market and where can we stand out.

Before opening up the canvas, I mapped about 10 competitors (some big, some small and trying to make a name like us) across content quality and community engagement. 

The platforms with real professional credibility (Elle, Vogue) feel cold and out of touch. The ones with relatable conent (Modern Salon, Behind the Chair) feel like content was being thrown at an undigestible speed.The gap confirmed the direction: warmer than the credible platforms, but more comprehensible than the high volume ones.

## To build credibility, we centered the brand around one idea: “For beauty pros, by beauty pros.”

The art director came to me with a starting point: hex codes, typography, and a low-fid wireframe. 


I wanted to create consistency across every platform and breakpoint. Getting five stakeholders (CEO, CMO, CDO, brand director, SEO manager) to agree on what that looks like in practice was its own project. I got better at reading whose feedback was about preference versus what actually mattered (still working on that one).

The palette and type choices were chosen to feel human, but restrained enough to feel credible. Not a fashion magazine, but also not a trade manual. The design system scales to work across the site and socials, but still has a recognizable personality.

## Developing a scalable design system to unify every touchpoint.

Copywriters needed to publish independently from day one. I built the component library in a specific order: tokens first, then base components, then composites and page templates. The structure allowed the team to ship new content without waiting on design or engineering each time.

The system includes:

- **Tokens** — color, type, spacing, and radius scales
- **Base components** — buttons, inputs, badges, tags
- **Composites** — product cards, review tiles, tables
- **Page templates** — landing, blog, review

## Designing the web experience.

The SEO strategy shaped the build order, and our data team identified which content we could push out fastest: higher-volume, keyword-friendly topics first. The richer editorial content moved to phase 2.

The goal was to translate the brand into a web experience meant creating something clean, intuitive, and content-first. We prioritized readability, balance, and flow for users to easily explore video tutorials, reviews, and articles without distraction.

## Results

The site soft launched in Q3 2025. Within the first 6 months:

- **4.2% add-to-cart rate** — above the 3.5% industry benchmark for new DTC brands
- **62% of sessions** reached the product page from the homepage
- **Zero support tickets** related to the checkout flow in the first two weeks
- The founder used the design system to ship two new editorial pages independently, without design support



## Reflection

As the solo designer on this project, the dev handoff was the hardest part. Fonts being used in the wrong places, components not scaling right, type sizes drifting. We worked through it, but I'd build a tighter QA loop earlier next time — not as a final check, but as part of the process.

The SEO first launch validated that we could build an audience without paid acquisition. Phase 2 will test whether that audience is the right one: beauty professionals who return for the depth of content, not just the search result.
