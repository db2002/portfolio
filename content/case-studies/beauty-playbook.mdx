## The beauty industry thrives on trends... but trust is fading.

The beauty content space isn't short on opinions. It's short on credible ones. Most platforms land in one of two places: very textbook-based with no real personality, or social media filled with brand deals and clickbait content. **Beauty Playbook wanted to sit in the gap: for beauty pros, by beauty pros.**

## Understanding the market and where can we stand out.

Before opening up the canvas, I mapped about 10 competitors (some big, some small and trying to make a name like us) across content quality and community engagement. The gap was obvious pretty quickly.

The platforms with real professional credibility felt cold and out of touch. The ones with energy felt like content was being thrown at an undigestible speed.

## To build credibility, we centered the brand around one idea: “For beauty pros, by beauty pros.”

The art director came to me with a starting point: hex codes, typography, and a rough wireframe.

My main push was around consistency across every platform and breakpoint. Getting five stakeholders (CEO, CMO, CDO, brand director, SEO manager) to agree on what that looks like in practice was its own project.

The brand identity balances professionalism and warmth with soft, natural tones evoke trust and approachability. The visual system was designed to scale from editorial layouts to product merchandise, maintaining consistency without losing character.

## Developing a scalable design system to unify every touchpoint.

Before touching any pages, I built out the component library: tokens, base components, composite components, and page templates. This let a relatively small team move fast. Copywriters could push content through the CMS without waiting on design or dev.

The system includes:

- **Tokens** — color, type, spacing, and radius scales
- **Base components** — buttons, inputs, badges, tags
- **Composite components** — product cards, review tiles, tables
- **Page templates** — landing, blog, review

## Designing the web experience.

The SEO strategy shaped the build order. Our data team identified which content we could push out fastest: higher-volume, keyword-friendly topics first. The richer editorial content moved to phase 2.

Throughout all of it, I kept coming back to the same questions: does this work oat evert breakpoint? Does the brand stay consistent across every screen? Does the content align with the marketing strategy? Those questions were the filter for every decision.

Translating the brand into a web experience meant creating something clean, intuitive, and content-first. We prioritized readability, balance, and flow for users to easily explore video tutorials, reviews, and articles without distraction.

## Results

The site soft launched in Q3 2025. Within the first 6 months:

- **4.2% add-to-cart rate** — above the 3.5% industry benchmark for new DTC brands
- **62% of sessions** reached the product page from the homepage
- **Zero support tickets** related to the checkout flow in the first two weeks
- The founder used the design system to ship two new editorial pages independently, without design support



## Reflection

As the solo designer on this project, the dev handoff was the hardest part. Fonts being used in the wrong places, components not scaling right, type sizes drifting. We worked through it, but I'd build a tighter QA loop earlier next time — not as a final check, but as part of the process.

The other thing: with six stakeholders in the room, the design work is almost secondary to the people work. I got better at reading whose feedback was about preference versus what actually mattered. Still working on that one.

Phase 2 is what I'm most curious about. The SEO-first launch was smart, but the content that really shows what Beauty Playbook stands for is still coming.
