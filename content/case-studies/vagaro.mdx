## Vagaro’s website was running on rigid templates. No system, just copies of the same page.

Vagaro operated on a small group of the exact same template, recycled and edited over and over again. Every  industry page followed the same pitch, in the same order: get found, get booked, get paid. However, it wasn’t a fool-proof solution. Some industries, like a therapist's practice, don't want to be discoverable on a public marketplace the way a salon does. But the template showed that pitch anyway, because the system had no way to say otherwise.

## Dev had tokens. Marketing had "brand colors." Nobody had agreed on how they connected.

The team before me didn't work in Figma, so there was no shared file connecting a token to what it should look like on the page. Dev knew the tokens existed. Marketing had their own names of “brand colors,” But nothing tied them together, so people ended up guessing at colors, font weights, and sizes page by page instead of pulling from one source everyone agreed on.

## Results

First page of Google, often #1–4. AEO visibility improved over the first couple of months, alongside backlinking and AEO-optimized FAQ content built on the same block structure.

<ThemedImage srcLight="/images/vagaro/vagaro-ranking-light.png" srcDark="/images/vagaro/vagaro-ranking-dark.png" alt="Vagaro's ranking on Google post-redesign" aspect="3.25/1" secondaryBg />

Within the first three quarters of the year, conversion rate rates almost doubled compared to before page updates in 2025 (data taken from Amplitude).
